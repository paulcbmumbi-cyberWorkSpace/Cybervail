The scam watch

Know the warning signs.

Scams change their story, but the pressure tactics often repeat. Learn the pattern so you can spot it sooner.

Examples for awareness
Not current incident reports
SHOW:
Message scam

“Your account will be closed”

A text or email claims there is a problem and pushes you to sign in through a link. The link may lead to a look-alike login page.

Red flagUrgency + an unexpected sign-in link.

Safer move: Open the official app or type the known website address yourself. Never share a one-time code.

Payment scam

“Pay a small fee to receive money”

A buyer, courier, or stranger says a fee is needed to release a parcel, prize, refund, or payment.

Red flagPaying first to receive something you did not expect.

Safer move: Verify directly with the service using its official contact details. Don’t pay to “unlock” a surprise.

Job scam

“You’re hired—send a fee today”

A recruiter promises quick earnings but asks for a registration fee, paid training, gift cards, or identity documents before a real interview.

Red flagUpfront payment or pressure to share sensitive details.

Safer move: Check the vacancy on the employer’s official careers page and verify the recruiter independently.

These are common scam patterns for education, not reports of a current campaign. We do not publish unverified incidents as live alerts.

Your quick guide

Pause before
you tap.

When a message asks you to act, take these four steps before clicking a link, opening a file, or sending money.

Already responded? Here’s what to do →
  1. 01

    Pause

    Unexpected urgency is a reason to slow down—not a reason to act faster.

  2. 02

    Check the sender

    Look closely at the full email address or phone number. A familiar name can be faked.

  3. 03

    Verify independently

    Contact the person or organisation through a number, app, or website you already trust.

  4. 04

    Keep secrets secret

    Never send passwords, PINs, recovery codes, or one-time verification codes to someone who contacts you.

!

No blame. Take action.

Clicked or shared something?

01

Stop replying. Don’t send more money or codes.

02

Change a shared password using the official app or website, then enable multi-factor authentication.

03

If money or payment details are involved, contact your bank or payment provider through its official channel immediately.

04

Report and block the account or message on the platform. Ask someone you trust for help if you feel unsure.

If a work or school account is involved, tell your IT or security team promptly.
